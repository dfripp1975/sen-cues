# The Book Club For One: cinematic hero concepts

Written with the 10K Websites skill (Phase 3), before Higgsfield was connected.
Nothing has been generated or spent yet. Pick one, connect Higgsfield, and the
next session starts at Phase 6 (the starting frame).

## What we know

- **What it is:** a real site with no usable photos, so every visual is generated.
  The site says the imagery is AI generated, or we swap in real photos later
  (decide this out loud before building).
- **Where the scroll page goes:** a cinematic front page that leads into the
  existing site (books, 20 tools, accounts). It does not replace the app.
- **The one call to action:** "Find my book" (search, then open its companion).
- **Phones** see a designed still image instead of the scrolling video, which
  plays on laptops and desktops.

## What readers actually say (research)

- "Nobody to talk to about it" once a book is finished.
- Clubs "meet on weekdays, when people work," and "fill up quickly."
- "I want to pick my own reading," not "feel obligated to read books I'm not
  interested in."
- The monthly pick "started to feel like a weight," "somewhat imprisoned."
- One wanted a club where "we discuss the books we're reading, not all the same one."

Copy below uses these words. Sources: r/books and r/kindle threads, Book Riot
"Our Reading Lives" essays, Goodreads author blog "Why I Quit My Book Group".

## Concept 1: Ink Drop (recommended)

**What the visitor sees:** one drop of deep indigo ink falls straight down
through clear, still water. As they scroll it hits, blooms, and unfurls into
slow soft clouds, with fine threads curling outward. Scroll up and it gathers
back into the drop.

**Where it rests:** the ink has settled into a calm, full-width cloud filling
the lower two thirds of the frame, soft edges, light coming through from above.
A full-bleed texture with nothing to crop, so text is safe on every screen.

**Why it works:** the drop falls as you scroll down (law 1 and 6), one subject
and one motion (law 2), fluid is the most forgiving subject AI renders (law 5),
and the ending is composed (law 4). Idea: a thought dropped into your head by a
book, spreading out. That is what a book club is for.

**Premise word:** *marginalia*, the notes readers write in the margins. The
site is the conversation in your margins.

**Signature element:** handwritten margin notes (drawn SVG) that write
themselves beside each section as you scroll, like someone annotating the page.

**Interactive moment:** press and hold to underline a sentence from a classic;
when the line finishes drawing, a spoiler-safe discussion question appears.

**Palette (from the footage):**
- canvas `#ECF1F3` (water light, never pure white)
- panel `#F7F9FA`
- ink `#1E2A6B`, deep ink `#0F1640` (text and headings)
- accent `#D9553F` (rare: the call to action and the underline only)
- secondary text `#4A5578`

**Type:** Libre Caslon Display (headlines, printed-book feel), Public Sans
(body), Geist Mono (small labels like chapter numbers), Caveat only inside the
drawn margin notes.

**Hero copy, one line per scroll band:**
1. "Finished a book and nobody to talk to?" (the drop falls)
2. "No meeting night. No book you didn't pick." (impact and bloom)
3. "Just your book, your chapter, your pace." (clouds unfurl)
4. Settle: "A book club for one." with **Find my book**

## Concept 2: One Seat

**What the visitor sees:** the camera glides straight down a tall wall of old
library shelves in early morning light, dust drifting through the sunbeams.
The spines are plain leather with no lettering.

**Where it rests:** looking down on a reading table: one open book, one cup,
one chair pulled out. Room left and right of the table for words.

**Why it works:** vertical descent, a clear arrival, and "one chair" says the
name without words. **Risk:** books and shelves are detailed objects, so fake
lettering can creep onto spines (law 5 and 12). Keep them distant and moving,
and expect one re-roll.

**Palette:** morning grey-green walls, pale oak, linen white, and a single
forest-green accent.

## Concept 3: Pages on the Water

**What the visitor sees:** loose blank pages drift down through a soft blue
dusk like falling leaves. Scrolling follows them down.

**Where it rests:** the pages land on a still lake, floating, with gentle
ripples spreading and the sky reflected between them.

**Why it works:** paper, light and water are all forgiving, the motion falls
with the scroll, and the mood is "read at your own pace." **Risk:** the page
backgrounds are busier, so caption legibility needs stronger scrims.

**Palette:** dusk blue, lilac haze, paper white, and a moonlit silver accent.
